It tells us that digital payments have become a part of everyday economic activity.

✅A tea seller receives a payment.
✅ A freelancer collects a fee.
✅ A family transfers money.
✅ A small retailer pays a supplier.

Individually, these transactions look ordinary.
Collectively, they reveal a significant change in how money moves through the economy.

For finance professionals, UPI offers several interesting areas to study:

• The reduction in friction during small-value transactions.
• The changing payment preferences of consumers.
• The potential for better transaction records.
• The opportunities and challenges of serving small businesses digitally.

However, transaction volume alone does not measure economic progress. ❌

We must also consider transaction value, active users, merchant adoption, failed transactions, fraud and the quality of access.

A high transaction count is an important indicator of digital adoption, but it is not a complete measure of financial inclusion.

The real question is not just how many payments India makes digitally.

It is how effectively digital payments help people participate in the formal economy.

For a marketer, a label is not just a compliance requirement. It is the first message a brand delivers on the shelf. So what happens when that message is the same for every product? 

 
That is a question worth asking as India shapes its approach to Front-of-Pack Labelling. 

Reformulating a product is a significant investment in R&D, sourcing, testing, packaging and consumer re-education. Brands make that investment when it helps them stand apart. But if a reformulated product carries the same “high” warning as one that has made no changes, what is the incentive for a manufacturer to invest? 

This is where the threshold, and the methodology behind it, deserve a closer look. Where the line is drawn, and whether a single “high” signal is the only way of expressing it, will decide which products get flagged and how consumers read them. A signal that only says a threshold has been crossed cannot show how far, or how much progress has been made towards it. 

 

That could discourage the very reformulation FOPL hopes to encourage. And for consumers, it could mean fewer better options on the shelf. 

 
How much has the threshold been exceeded? How does one product compare with another? What does the number mean for the portion actually being consumed? 

These questions matter because food choices rarely happen in isolation. Serving size, frequency of consumption and the broader diet all shape what a nutrient level means. Similar products can have different nutritional profiles even when they carry the same warning. 

 
This is where a little personalisation goes a long way: helping consumers see what a product means for their portion, their diet and their household. 

That requires clear communication on the pack, access to meaningful nutritional information, and sustained efforts to build nutrition literacy. 

Because the best consumer choice is an informed one. Empowering consumers is not about telling them what to choose. It is about giving them the tools to understand their choices better. 

Perhaps that is the opportunity for FOPL: not simply to create a more visible label, but a more informed decision-making process, one that also rewards the brands doing the work to improve.

Protect it. Lead with it. Every single time.

c. Make sure the AI Matches your intent in the output.

Don't open AI first.
Write your messy, unfiltered first draft by hand. Even 5 bullet points. Even half a paragraph. Then hand it to AI and say: "Clean this up. Keep my voice. Don't change the ideas."

AI becomes your editor, instead of you being AI's medium!

Your best posts. 
Your most-used phrases.
